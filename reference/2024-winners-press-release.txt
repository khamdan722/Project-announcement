صعوبة الدلالات التشخيصية في تحديد المتغيرات المترتبة على التوصيف الجزيئي: دراسة شاملة لــ 4577 عائلة.
فريق بحثي من مستشفى الملك فيصل التخصصي.
This study discusses the challenges faced in identifying and interpreting genetic variants in patients with Mendelian diseases. Mendelian diseases are inherited disorders caused by mutations in a single gene. We studied a large group of 4577 families with Mendelian diseases and encountered various scenarios where identifying the disease-causing genetic variants was difficult. These challenges fell into different categories, such as novel disorders that had not been previously seen, difficulties in mapping the variants to specific genes, and cases involving complex patterns of inheritance. On average, we estimated that there was a 34.3% chance of encountering at least one of these challenges when analyzing the genetic data of patients. Importantly, we found that eliminating challenges which were solely related to the sequencing process, the diagnostic yield could potentially increase by ~71%. In other words, by considering a broader range of factors beyond just the genetic sequencing data, they were able to improve the chances of identifying the cause of the disease. We also applied our approach to a separate group of 314 patients who had previously received negative clinical exome or genome reports. Using our comprehensive approach, we were able to identify the likely causative genetic variant in 54.5% of these cases. The study emphasizes the importance of taking a holistic approach to diagnosing diseases, considering various challenges beyond just sequencing technologies. By sharing our experiences and the lessons we learned, we hope to improve the effectiveness of undiagnosed disease programs globally.

التحول للمضادات الحيوية الفموية في حالات بكتيريا الدم سالبة الجرام: تجربة سريرية عشوائية مفتوحة.
فريق بحثي من مؤسسة حمد الطبية  (قطر)– جامعة الكويت - جامعة ميديبول إسطنبول.
We evaluated oral (PO) switch in patients with Enterobacterales blood stream infection (E-BSI), compared with continuing intravenous (IV) therapy.This was a pragmatic, multicenter, open, randomized, non-inferiority trial. Main inclusion criteria were age ≥18 years, monomicrobial E-BSI caused by a strain susceptible to oral beta-lactams, quinolones or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, completion of 3–5 days of microbiologically active IV therapy, being afebrile and hemodynamically stable for ≥48 hours, and absence of an uncontrolled source of infection. Pregnancy, endocarditis, and neurological infections were excluded. Permuted block randomization was stratified by urinary source of bacteremia. Subjects were randomized to continuing IV or switching to oral therapy. Agent and duration of therapy were determined by the treating physicians. The primary endpoint was treatment failure, defined as a composite of need for additional antimicrobial therapy with ≥1 microbiologically active agent before resolution of infection, and death, microbiological relapse, or infection-related re-admission within 90 days. Non-inferiority of switch to oral would be concluded if the upper limit of the 95% confidence interval (CI) for the difference in the proportion with treatment failure between the two groups in the modified intention to treat (mITT) population is ›10%.we enrolled 174 subjects with mean age 56.6 years. 51.1% were females and 60.3% had a urinary source. subjects were randomly allocated to the IV Group (n = 85) and PO Group (n = 89). The most frequent pathogen was E. coli (66.7%). Treatment failure was documented in 28.2% and 24.7% of all subjects in the IV Group and Oral Group, respectively, and in 21/82 (25.6%) and 18/83 (21.7%) in the mITT population. The proportions of subjects with any adverse events (AE), leading to treatment discontinuation were comparable.we concluded that in patients with E-BSI, oral switch after initial IV antimicrobial therapy, clinical stability and source control, is non-inferior to continuing IV therapy.

فريق بحثي من كليفلاند كلينيك - أبوظبي
In our study, we aimed to improve care for patients with testicular torsion, a condition requiring urgent treatment to save the testicle. We introduced the TWIST score and implemented a collaborative, multidisciplinary approach termed ”Code torsion”, with hospital-wide announcement, to expedite and coordinate patient care.Analyzing data before and after our interventions, we found significant improvements, including cutting time to shift to operating room from 200 minutes to just un 60 minutes leading to better outcomes for patients. Utilizing multidisciplinary teams enabled more efficient resource allocation, resulting in faster examinations, imaging, and surgeries. Time-to-event intervals notably decreased, with reduced time to examination, imaging, and surgery. Furthermore, the post-implementation group showed higher testicular salvage rates compared to the pre-implementation cohort, indicating improved outcomes. This highlights the effectiveness of our approach in optimizing resource usage and enhancing patient care.Our study underscores the value of collaborative, evidence-based interventions in optimizing care for patients with acute scrotal pain. Through the integration of multidisciplinary teams and an accelerated care pathway, we achieved notable improvements in efficiency, resource utilization, and ultimately, patient outcome

الابتكار في علاج الالتهاب الرئوي من خلال مراجعة الالتزام بالإرشادات، وتوقع الوفيات باستخدام تعلم الآلة القابل للتفسير.
فريق بحثي من جامعة الشارقة ومستشفى القاسمي ومستشفى توام
Pneumonia is a very common type of infection that affects the lungs. There are many types of pneumonia, but those caused by bacteria tend to be more serious. Globally, a number of associations review available evidence regarding pneumonia and release guidelines to assist doctors in managing pneumonia patients. This is important as using antibiotics inappropriately can make them less effective since bacteria become resistant to them. In this study, all bacterial pneumonia cases from 2016 to 2021 in Al Qassimi hospital were evaluated to determine whether doctors were following the guidelines set forth by the American Thoracic Society for pneumonia care. Additionally, the data collected was used to develop a machine learning model (named PneumoMLPred) that can help predict which cases would die from pneumonia, as this would help flag those patients to the doctors for more aggressive treatment and monitoring. 783 cases were analysed and the results showed doctors not sticking to the guidelines’ recommendations. They were not ordering all the required tests, establishing the cause of the pneumonia, or using the right antibiotics at the right doses. Additionally, doctors were using some very powerful and restricted antibiotics without establishing or trying to establish the need for them (these antibiotics can cover against some very resistant and tough bacteria). Second, the model developed (PneumoMLPred) model showed excellent ability in recognising and understanding which factors can predict death due to pneumonia. The performance of the model was at least at the same level if not higher compared to other internationally-developed models. All in all, this study showed that doctors need to stick more closely to pneumonia guidelines to make sure they are providing the best possible care and that the PneumoMLPred model can be implemented locally to help with identifying high-risk patients and improve pneumonia care.

فعالية العلاجات الدوائية في إدارة المثانة مفرطة النشاط لدى الأطفال والمراهقين: مراجعة منهجية وتحليل تلوي.
فريق بحثي من مستشفى الجليلة للأطفال ومستشفى كليفلاند كلينك – أبوظبي وجامعة الشارقة
Overactive bladder is the most common urination problem in children and adolescents, approximately present in 15-20% of them. The main feature is the sudden feeling of desire to urinate. It is essential to identify and treat it in childhood so that it does not persist into adulthood with further complications. Unlike adults, there is not enough evidence for the medications used in this condition in children. The only approved drug for use is Oxybutynin, which is known to cause many undesirable side effects in children; thus, other types of medications were sought. To this date, there are no good quality studies that investigated the efficacy of all the currently available medications for overactive bladder in children and adolescents, which include Anticholinergics, Mirabegron and Botulinum toxin. Thus, this study aimed to investigate the efficacy of these medications by searching electronically for trials all around the world that addressed this condition’s management. The findings of the eligible trials included in this study were assessed for quality and their data was pooled in a certain methodological way to produce better understanding of the efficacy in terms of improving the symptoms and complete resolution of the condition. The overall finding is that mirabegron is more efficacious than certain anticholinergics for improving some outcomes. There is a concern regarding how truly good are these findings in terms of translating them to real life practice, with such concern physicians might not feel confident enough to change their management. Thus, more trials are needed to conclude more precise results and to increase the level of confidence in the produced findings. It is recommended for future studies to be conducted in a better design, and to include side effects comparison.

Ramadan fasting in people with diabetes and kidney disease
Many Muslims with chronic conditions such as diabetes and chronic kidney disease are keen to fast Ramadan despite the potential medical-religious exemptions. During Ramadan fasting, the restrictions on food and fluids intake, the sleeping pattern and the possible change in medications timing are considered a potential risk for dehydration, low or high blood glucose levels, low blood pressure, and/or further kidney damage for people with diabetes and kidney disease. Consequently, all diabetes and Ramadan guidelines consider fasting in people with diabetes and chronic kidney disease a very high-risk. Furthermore, Ramadan 2020 coincided with the coronavirus epidemic, making it one of the most challenging fasting times for Muslims around the world. Unfortunately, limited studies are available that provides information regarding the safety of fasting in people with diabetes and kidney disease.
This is the largest study in people with diabetes and kidney disease, designed to observe fasting patterns, practices, and outcomes during Ramadan. 6736 persons with diabetes took part in part in the study from several Arab countries as well as many other countries. 707 of them had kidney disease as well. The results show that compared to those with diabetes but without kidney disease, less people were able to fast (75%). Episodes of low as well as high blood sugar level were more frequent especially among people with type 1 diabetes. In some, these episodes necessitated visits to the emergency department and/or hospitalization. The study highlights that while many were able to safely fast, some were at increased risks to their health. Furthermore, the study highlights the need for Ramadan focused education and better risk assessment for those wishing to fast. A new study is about to start to evaluate the risks in different levels of kidney disease in this group.
